Film One · Adventure

THE SECRET
LIFE OF
WALTER MITTY

Directed by Ben Stiller

Walter Mitty is a negative assets manager at LIFE Magazine who daydreams his way through a life he's too afraid to actually live. When a crucial photo negative goes missing, he's forced out of his head and into the world — across Greenland, Iceland, and Afghanistan — discovering that the life he imagined was smaller than the one waiting for him.

It's a film about the courage it takes to stop dreaming and start moving. Stiller wraps that idea in some of the most stunning landscape cinematography of the decade, set to a score that makes you want to buy a one-way ticket somewhere cold.

"To see the world, things dangerous to come to, to see behind walls, to draw closer, to find each other and to feel. That is the purpose of life."

Film Two · Psychological Thriller

SE7EN

Directed by David Fincher

Two detectives — one jaded and days from retirement, one bright-eyed and newly arrived — hunt a serial killer whose murders each enact one of the seven deadly sins. Fincher builds a city that feels diseased, perpetually wet, where darkness isn't atmosphere but fact.

Se7en is a masterclass in controlled dread. It doesn't shock you — it slowly, methodically convinces you that the world is exactly as bad as you feared. The ending is among the most devastating in cinema history. You already know it. It still destroys you.

"Ernest Hemingway once wrote, 'The world is a fine place and worth fighting for.' I agree with the second part."

Film Three · Drama

WHIPLASH

Directed by Damien Chazelle

Andrew Neiman is a first-year drummer at the most prestigious music conservatory in America. Terence Fletcher is the conductor who will either make him or break him — possibly both. What follows is one of cinema's most brutal examinations of obsession, greatness, and the cost of demanding the absolute most from yourself.

Chazelle edited this film to feel like jazz — unpredictable, relentless, building to something you don't see coming. The final ten minutes are as good as anything put on screen in the last thirty years. You will not breathe.

"There are no two words in the English language more harmful than 'good job.'"
